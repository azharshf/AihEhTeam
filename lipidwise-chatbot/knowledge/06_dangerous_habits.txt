DANGEROUS HABITS FOR USERS WITH DYSLIPIDEMIA

These habits can worsen dyslipidemia and increase cardiovascular risk:

DIETARY HABITS:
- Eating large amounts of fried food regularly (goreng, deep-fried snacks).
- Consuming high amounts of saturated fat from fatty meats, full-fat dairy, coconut-based dishes.
- Drinking sugary beverages daily (sweetened tea, sodas, fruit juices with added sugar).
- Eating refined carbohydrates excessively (white rice, white bread, pastries).
- Consuming trans fats from processed foods, margarine, and commercially baked goods.
- Skipping meals then overeating, which can spike triglyceride levels.
- Late-night eating or supper habits with heavy meals.

LIFESTYLE HABITS:
- Smoking or vaping. Smoking directly lowers HDL-C and accelerates plaque buildup.
- Excessive alcohol consumption. Heavy drinking significantly raises triglycerides.
- Physical inactivity. Sedentary lifestyle reduces HDL-C and worsens overall lipid profile.
- Chronic sleep deprivation (less than 6 hours regularly).
- Unmanaged chronic stress.

MEDICATION HABITS:
- Stopping prescribed medication without doctor's advice.
- Not following up on blood test results.
- Self-medicating with supplements without medical guidance.
- Ignoring doctor's recommendation for lipid panel testing.

RISKY COMBINATIONS:
- Smoking + high LDL-C = significantly increased heart attack risk.
- Diabetes + high triglycerides = compounded cardiovascular risk.
- Obesity + sedentary lifestyle + poor diet = accelerated dyslipidemia progression.
- Family history of heart disease + unmonitored lipid levels = silent risk accumulation.
